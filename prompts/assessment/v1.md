You review a candidate functional requirement that was reconstructed from a
Pull Request, and decide whether it can be accepted.

## Necessary conditions

A requirement cannot be accepted if any of these fails. They are not traded
off against good wording:

1. Evidence fidelity - every element is supported by the title or the body of
   the Pull Request. Any unsupported channel, limit, format, technology, or
   actor makes the requirement unacceptable.
2. Functional nature - it states a behaviour, capability, or response of the
   system, not a refactoring, a build change, or a pure quality attribute.
3. Faithfulness - it represents the behaviour the Pull Request actually
   describes, without altering its meaning.
4. Singularity - it expresses one main obligation.
5. Unambiguity - one reasonable interpretation of the required behaviour.
6. Verifiability - one can determine by observation, test, or analysis whether
   the behaviour is satisfied.

## Further quality criteria

Clarity, completeness relative to the available evidence, necessity of every
element, correct level of abstraction, and independence from implementation
details. Weakness here leads to revision, not to automatic rejection.

## Historical requirements

When previously validated requirements are provided, use them to judge
consistency with the existing corpus. A candidate may be correct with respect
to its Pull Request and still overlap or conflict with earlier requirements.
Report what you observe in the issues; the relation does not by itself
determine the decision.

## Decision

- ACCEPT - every necessary condition holds and the requirement is usable as
  validated.
- REVISE - the problems can be fixed using the evidence already available,
  for example by removing unsupported details, splitting merged behaviours,
  clarifying wording, or correcting the level of abstraction.
- REJECT - another rewrite would not help, because the evidence does not
  support any functional requirement without arbitrary assumptions.

Choose REVISE when a better sentence is reachable from the same evidence, and
REJECT when it is not.

## Output

Reply with a single JSON object and nothing else. All four lists must be
present; use an empty list when there is nothing to report. When the decision
is REVISE, revision_instructions must be specific enough to be acted on
without further explanation.

{
  "decision": "ACCEPT" | "REVISE" | "REJECT",
  "issues": ["<problem observed>"],
  "unsupported_claims": ["<element not supported by the evidence>"],
  "missing_information": ["<functional information present in the PR but absent from the requirement>"],
  "revision_instructions": ["<concrete instruction for the next attempt>"]
}
